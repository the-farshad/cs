**Linear algebra** is the math of **vectors** and **matrices** — and it is the
engine behind computer graphics, machine learning, physics simulations, and data
analysis. The key insight is that a matrix is not just a grid of numbers; it is a
*transformation* of space.

## Vectors

A **vector** is a quantity with direction and magnitude, written as a column of
numbers like $\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$. You can **add** vectors
component-wise and **scale** them by a number. In code, a vector is just an
array — a point, a velocity, a color, or a row of features.

## Matrices as transformations

A 2x2 matrix $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ transforms every
point in the plane. The trick: it sends the basis vector $\hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$
to the first column $\begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix}$, and $\hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
to the second column $\begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}$. Every other point
follows along linearly.

Different entries produce familiar effects:
- **Scaling:** $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ doubles everything.
- **Rotation:** $\begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{bmatrix}$ spins the plane.
- **Shear:** $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ slides the top sideways.

## Dot Product and Matrix Multiplications

The **dot product** of two vectors $u \cdot v$ measures how much they "point in
the same direction." It's the sum of their products: $u_1v_1 + u_2v_2$.

**Matrix multiplication** is really about **composition**. If matrix $A$ rotates
and matrix $B$ scales, then the product $BA$ is the single transformation that
rotates *then* scales. In code, this is how we chain graphics transforms or
neural network layers.

## The determinant

The **determinant** $(ad - bc)$ measures how much the transformation scales **area**.
- $\text{det} = 1$: area preserved (e.g., a pure rotation).
- $\text{det} = 0$: the unit square collapses to a line — the matrix is **not invertible** and information is lost.
- $\text{det} < 0$: the plane is **flipped** over (orientation reversed).

## Takeaways

- Vectors are directed quantities; a matrix transforms whole vectors at once.
- A 2x2 matrix is defined by where it sends the basis vectors $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$.
- Matrix multiplication corresponds to composing linear transformations.
- The determinant is the area scale factor; $\text{det} = 0$ means information is lost.
