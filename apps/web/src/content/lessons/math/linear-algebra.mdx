**Linear algebra** is the math of **vectors** and **matrices** — and it is the
engine behind computer graphics, machine learning, physics simulations, and data
analysis. The key insight is that a matrix is not just a grid of numbers; it is a
*transformation* of space.

## Vectors

A **vector** is a quantity with direction and magnitude, written as a column of
numbers like `[3, 2]`. You can **add** vectors component-wise and **scale** them by a
number. In code, a vector is just an array — a point, a velocity, a color, or a row
of features.

## Matrices as transformations

A 2x2 matrix `[[a, b], [c, d]]` transforms every point in the plane. The trick: it
sends the basis vector `î = [1, 0]` to the first column `[a, c]`, and `ĵ = [0, 1]` to
the second column `[b, d]`. Every other point follows along, because

`M · [x, y] = x · (first column) + y · (second column)`

Different entries produce familiar effects:

- **Scaling:** `[[2, 0], [0, 2]]` doubles everything.
- **Rotation:** `[[cos θ, −sin θ], [sin θ, cos θ]]` spins the plane.
- **Shear:** `[[1, 1], [0, 1]]` slides the top sideways.

## The determinant

The **determinant** `ad − bc` measures how much the transformation scales **area**.

- `det = 1`: area preserved (a pure rotation).
- `det = 0`: the square collapses to a line — the matrix is **not invertible** and
  information is lost.
- `det < 0`: the plane is **flipped** over (orientation reversed).

## Why it matters for CS

Graphics pipelines multiply matrices to move and rotate objects. Neural networks are
chains of matrix multiplications. Search engines and recommender systems represent
data as vectors and compare them with dot products. Once you see matrices as
transformations, all of this clicks into place.

## Takeaways

- Vectors are directed quantities you can add and scale; a matrix transforms whole
  vectors at once.
- A 2x2 matrix is defined by where it sends the basis vectors `î` and `ĵ`.
- The determinant is the area scale factor; `det = 0` means the matrix is not
  invertible.
